## Why it lands

**The gap it fills.** Almost everyone has done it: a hand goes up across the
street, you wave back with a smile — and then you realise the wave was meant
for the person standing right behind you. English has words for the wave
itself, for embarrassment, and for mistaken identity, but no single word for
that exact little social misfire. *Miswave* names the moment, not just the
feeling, so a reader recognises it in one reading.

**Sayable.** It is built from two sounds English speakers already own: the
prefix *mis-* (mistake, misread, misfire) and *wave*. It rolls off the tongue
in two syllables, with the stress falling naturally on the first: MIS-wave.

**Spellable.** There is only one plausible spelling. The *mis-* prefix is one
of the most productive in English, and *wave* is spelled exactly as it
sounds, so anyone who hears the word once can write it down.

**Memorable.** The word tells its own story: *mis-* signals "wrong" before
the second syllable even arrives, so the meaning is half-guessed before the
definition is read. It sits beside familiar cousins — *misread*, *mistake*,
*misstep* — which makes it feel like a word English simply forgot to grow.
